Built to support rigorous thinking, with a clear view of what the material can and cannot tell you.

Can I start with a report instead of raw data?

Yes. Start with existing reports to extract findings and examine claims. Without underlying data, the assessment considers the material supplied; it is not an independent replication of the original statistics.

Does this replace the researcher?

No. Insights Elevator assists analysis, assessment, synthesis and recommendation development. Researchers review the evidence and retain responsibility for interpretation and decisions.

What does an evidence assessment tell me?

It assesses a claim against the evidence available in the project. Verdicts distinguish support, caution, unsupported claims and insufficient information. They do not guarantee that a claim is universally true.

What can I take into the stakeholder meeting?

Develop an editable narrative and PowerPoint presentation, with findings and recommendations connected to the working research. Review the reasoning, caveats and proposed actions before sharing.

Start where your project needs you. These modules help you move from research material to a considered interpretation and a practical next step.

01 / WORKSPACE

Import & data setup

Bring survey datasets, statistical outputs, transcripts and reports into your project. Review variables, labels and data setup, and connect questionnaires and codebooks to the data they describe.

Data files · Questionnaires · Codebooks
02 / WORKSPACE

Research quality

Examine the methodology and evidence available in the project. Identify limitations, missing information and claims that need qualification before they shape the interpretation.

Methodology · Evidence checks · Caveats
03 / WORKSPACE

Cross tabs

Explore how responses differ across groups. Build tables with the relevant bases, weights and comparisons, then carry useful results into the findings workflow.

Segments · Comparisons · Tables
04 / WORKSPACE

Analytics

Investigate relationships, patterns and potential drivers in your data. Review the analytical output and its assumptions before deciding what the result means for the research question.

Patterns · Relationships · Drivers
05 / WORKSPACE

Qualitative coding

Develop and apply a codebook to interviews and focus groups. Review coded excerpts, explore themes and assess repeat-coding stability while keeping the original conversation in view.

Codebooks · Themes · Source excerpts
06 / WORKSPACE

Secondary analysis

Extract facts, findings and stated insights from existing reports. Examine the supporting material and distinguish what a document reports from the interpretation built around it.

Reports · Extracted findings · Source context
07 / WORKSPACE

Findings

Bring observations from reports, imported tables, cross tabs, analytics and coding into one working collection. Review the source and supporting evidence before using a finding in the wider story.

Source links · Evidence review · Working findings
08 / WORKSPACE

Insights & synthesis

Connect related findings to develop a clearer interpretation. Move beyond individual observations to explore the tension, implication and relevance to the decision at hand.

Connected findings · Interpretation · Implications
09 / WORKSPACE

Recommendations

Turn insights into proposed actions. Define an owner, timeline, measures and assumptions, and examine the reasoning behind a recommendation before committing to the next step.

Actions · Owners · Measures
10 / WORKSPACE

Reports & presentations

Shape the research into an editable narrative and presentation. Bring findings, insights and recommendations together, with the qualifications stakeholders need to understand the story.

Narrative · Reports · PowerPoint

The depth of an assessment depends on the material supplied. Researchers review the outputs and retain responsibility for interpretation and decisions.
