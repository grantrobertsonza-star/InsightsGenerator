SOUTH AFRICAN SOFT-DRINKS U&A — SYNTHETIC TEST PACK

Purpose
A fictional client brief, a fully numbered and coded questionnaire, a master codebook and three synthetic respondent datasets for testing research analysis and reporting. No actual people were surveyed. No findings represent actual brand performance, South African market shares or population estimates. Karoo Drinks and Karoo Fizz are fictional study identities.

MAIN RESEARCH INPUTS
1. UA_Soft_Drinks_Client_Brief.docx — fictional client-to-agency brief.
2. UA_Soft_Drinks_Questionnaire.docx — administered order, Q1–Q139, full question wording, coded options and routing. Individual respondents only answer their applicable routes and allocated modules.
3. UA_Soft_Drinks_Master_Codebook.xlsx — physical variables, question IDs, complete labels, response codes, routing, periods, units and study definitions.
4. UA_Soft_Drinks_Wave_1_5000.csv
5. UA_Soft_Drinks_Wave_2_5000.csv
6. UA_Soft_Drinks_Wave_3_5000.csv

Each data file contains exactly 5,000 respondent rows and the same 600 columns, including administrative fields and response-state indicators. There are 15,000 unique people across the three independent cross-sections. This is not a longitudinal panel. CSV is UTF-8 and includes a header row.

No JSON, custom semantic mapping or separate dashboard mapping file is required to interpret the research inputs. The codebook explicitly links physical column names to the final printed question numbers. Column-name prefixes are stable identifiers and must not be assumed to equal the printed Q number.

SCOPE AND PERIODS
Adults aged 18+ living in South Africa, including never-users, lapsed users and recent users. Carbonated soft drinks include regular and low/no-sugar products. Use the questionnaire's exact category definition and exclusions; excluded beverages can be substitutes without being counted as category use.

Wave 1: synthetic reference date 1 April 2026; preceding calendar quarter January–March 2026.
Wave 2: synthetic reference date 1 July 2026; preceding calendar quarter April–June 2026.
Wave 3: synthetic reference date 1 October 2026; preceding calendar quarter July–September 2026.
Other recall and future periods are defined in the questionnaire and codebook.

WEIGHTS AND SIZING
Analysis weights are synthetic and normalised to mean approximately 1 within each wave. They are not calibrated to an actual South African population.
The population-expansion weights sum to a FICTIONAL planning universe of 1,000,000 adults within EACH wave. This invented universe exists only to test absolute population/volume/value calculations. It is not a real population estimate. Do not sum the three wave universes into a 3,000,000-person population.

INTERPRETATION
All nine conceptual model sections have supporting inputs across each wave. Ordinary brand-association, attribute and situation-recall modules are split-sample; not every respondent is asked every module. Apply assignment and eligibility bases before calculating results.
Keep purchase separate from consumption, consideration separate from historical use, and choice intention separate from buyer-only repurchase intention. Repeated brand shopping-trip counts may overlap when several brands are bought on a trip; do not force these to sum to total trips or label them volume market share.
Use response-state fields and codebook routes. A blank routed/not-shown answer is not automatically a zero or No. Any derived logical-zero rule must be supported by confirmed non-use and matching periods.

OPTIONAL TEST REFERENCE — NOT A RESPONDENT DATA INPUT
QA_Expected_Metrics.csv supplies calculated reference results for checking report calculations. Do not upload it as another respondent dataset or treat it as survey responses.
This Read Me and QA reference are convenience materials, not special mapping files required by the app.

This pack is designed to exercise analysis and mapping. It does not demonstrate that any particular app importer, classifier or dashboard has passed its own acceptance tests. Real fieldwork still requires questionnaire piloting, ethics/privacy procedures, sample design and category-specific review.
